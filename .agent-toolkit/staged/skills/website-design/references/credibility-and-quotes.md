# Credibility and quotes

Use company proof and human voices to support the site's promise where real evidence exists. Plan their placement with the visitor story: an early concise quote can strengthen the opening hook; a fuller quote section can follow a product demonstration; a relevant quote near an inquiry action can address a specific hesitation. Choose useful placements rather than repeating the same carousel throughout the page.

## Content and attribution

- Use supplied or approved quotes with the speaker's name, role, company, and source or approval context. Keep attribution attached to the quote during every state change. Favor a specific workflow, experience, or outcome over generic praise; do not turn a speaker's words into a stronger claim.
- Keep customer testimonials, team perspectives, employer experience, education, and partner/customer logos distinguishable. A previous employer logo does not establish endorsement or customer status.
- Preserve supplied company logo colors and proportions. Choose a compatible surface when a logo needs contrast instead of automatically applying grayscale or inversion. Use alternate official artwork only when appropriate.
- When real quotes are unavailable, prepare clearly marked quote slots in the design room. Use a neutral avatar or initials slot and explicit labels such as “Approved quote goes here” and “Name · role · company.” Do not invent a testimonial, person, headshot, or customer relationship. Carry the missing content into the handoff; do not publish placeholders as social proof.

## Visual treatment

Favor an open, clean composition: readable editorial type, deliberate line lengths, calm spacing, and restrained attribution. The quote should lead. Avoid defaulting to boxed testimonial grids or decorative rating badges. Reserve enough space for the longest approved quote so switching people does not move the surrounding page, while keeping mobile spacing proportional.

One useful reference is [Othello's careers quote selector](https://www.othello.ai/careers): a compact row of portraits, a clear selected person, a large quote, and a quiet name/role line. Study its actual behavior before claiming a faithful reproduction. Its content and portraits belong to the source and are not testimonials for another site.

Creative alternatives include a compact quote beside company proof, two quotes that cycle in staggered rhythm, or a wider selector with role/company context. Keep the evidence and attribution legible as one component; avoid motion that competes with reading.

## Interaction and motion

People selectors need semantic buttons or tabs, accessible speaker labels, a visible selected state, keyboard operation, and usable touch targets. Update quote and attribution together without stealing focus. Give automatic cycling enough dwell time to read the longest quote; expose pause/resume, pause on hover or keyboard focus, suspend while offscreen or the page is hidden, and stop automatic advancement after manual selection until the user resumes. Avoid announcing every automatic change to screen readers.

Respect reduced motion with a calm, manually selectable state and no automatic cycling. A brief fade or restrained transition may suit ordinary motion; preserve readable text throughout. Verify all selections, attribution correspondence, pause/resume, manual takeover, reduced motion, longest-quote wrapping, and mobile layout. Original source autoplay, timing, replay, and accessibility are evidence to inspect, not behavior to assume.
